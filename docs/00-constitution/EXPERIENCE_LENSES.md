# Experience lenses

Status: DRAFT

These are the questions used to judge future design choices before implementation detail.

## 1. Memory

Will the player remember this?

A place, weapon, road, NPC, enemy, quest or journey should have enough duration/context to become part of the player's internal story.

If everything is replaced immediately, nothing becomes memory.

## 2. Distance

Does physical distance still mean something?

Travel systems should reduce friction through knowledge and earned infrastructure without erasing the world.

If the optimal interface is always a teleport menu, geography has failed.

## 3. Return

Does the design create reasons to come back?

Old regions, towns and NPCs should remain in the player's life.

Return creates familiarity and lets progression be felt against remembered danger.

## 4. Rhythm

Does the game alternate intensity and quiet?

Continuous danger is exhausting.

Continuous safety is dull.

Continuous reward becomes noise.

The desired experience breathes.

## 5. Commitment

Do actions sometimes require commitment?

A journey, cast, dungeon run or long quest should sometimes ask the player to make a choice and live with it for more than a few seconds.

Convenience is not automatically good design.

## 6. Recovery

Does returning to safety feel different from being outside?

Inns, camps and towns matter more when wilderness creates attrition and distance.

Recovery should provide relief rather than busywork.

## 7. Accumulation

Does character growth feel like accumulated history?

Abilities, profession skill, routes, faction standing, gear, knowledge and quests should layer over time.

The character should feel older, not merely numerically larger.

## 8. Texture

Does ordinary play contain small decisions?

Which road?
One more pull?
Drink now?
Cut through the forest?
Carry this item?
Return home?
Stay at the inn until morning?

A slow game survives through texture.

## 9. Restraint

Can the game allow nothing to happen?

Quiet is not missing content.

The world needs enough restraint that discovering something still matters.

## 10. Respect for time

Is the game slow because the experience has breadth and depth, or because the player is being delayed?

Long travel can be meaningful.

Long quest chains can be meaningful.

A dungeon night can be meaningful.

Arbitrary deletion of progress, unnecessary waiting and repeated chores are not made good by calling them hardcore.
