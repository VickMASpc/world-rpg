# First 15 hours — lived-experience storyboard

Status: ACCEPTED FOR GROUNDING — CONTENT NAMES AND EXACT TIMINGS PROVISIONAL

This storyboard is not a quest list, tutorial script, zone checklist or production map.

It answers a narrower and more important question:

**What should the first fifteen hours of living as a World RPG character feel like?**

The current P4 calibration draft reaches approximately level 10 around 13.7 hours. That remains a provisional pacing model, not a requirement that every player reaches a particular level at a particular minute.

The storyboard therefore describes an opening character who is roughly in the first ten levels after fifteen hours, while preserving wandering, profession time, failure, different routes and unfinished work.

All place labels below are functional placeholders. They must not become canon names merely because this document exists.

---

# The province roles

The first province needs at least these geographic roles:

## A — home settlement

A small settlement where the character begins to form routine.

It should provide only a subset of civilization:

- inn / safe stopping point;
- basic supplies;
- repair;
- several local NPC relationships;
- one or more class/profession touchpoints appropriate to the final content plan;
- a reason to return after the player has already left it.

It is not a complete city.

## B — local fields / woods / working land

The nearest wild edge.

This is where early combat can be readable and low-pressure while the player learns:

- distance;
- pulling;
- recovery;
- resource persistence;
- terrain reading;
- gathering;
- the practical difference between road and off-road travel.

## C — main road

A reliable route leaving the home settlement.

It should gradually become familiar enough that the player can walk part of it without opening a map.

## D — river crossing / bridge / fork

A strong navigation landmark where route decisions begin.

It should connect at least:

- the safe/main route;
- a riskier shortcut or side path;
- a route toward the regional settlement.

## E — dangerous visible place

A ruin, ridge, cave mouth, occupied fort, predator territory or equivalent place visible early but clearly inappropriate for the new character.

The player should understand:

**I can physically go there, but I should not yet.**

This is not an invisible level wall.

It establishes that the authored world has objective danger independent of the player's level.

## F — remote local refuge

An inn, outpost, farmstead, ferry house or similarly small safe point beyond the first comfortable local radius.

It has fewer services than a town but changes expedition reach.

## G — regional settlement

The first place that makes the world feel larger than the starting area.

It should not be reached in the opening minutes.

It has a broader service identity than the home settlement and becomes another place the player can stage from without replacing home immediately.

## H — unfinished deeper direction

A road, pass, distant structure, mine, forest edge, valley or other route visibly leading beyond what the first fifteen hours can exhaust.

The province must feel larger than the player's completed checklist.

# Hour 0–1.5 — belonging before departure

The game does not begin by throwing ten systems at the player.

The opening should establish:

- where the character currently lives;
- who provides ordinary support;
- what the nearest road looks like;
- where the first wild edge begins;
- that equipment, money and supplies are limited;
- that leaving safety is a physical act.

Possible lived events:

- talk to a few people rather than vacuuming every NPC;
- receive or already possess a small number of meaningful intentions;
- buy or receive an initial class tool/weapon;
- perform one local task that requires leaving the immediate settlement;
- encounter ordinary enemies without turning the opening into a combat arena;
- return with resources partially spent rather than automatically reset;
- discover that repair/selling/rest are located in actual places.

The player should not yet be shown every profession, travel system, faction, dungeon type or class subsystem.

### Memory target

After 90 minutes, the player should be able to answer without UI:

- where the inn is;
- which direction the main road leaves town;
- where ordinary danger begins;
- which local person they expect to speak to again.

### Anti-pattern check

If the player has already replaced several pieces of equipment, cleared a dozen marked objectives and unlocked a full action bar, the opening is moving too fast.

---

# Hour 1.5–3 — first real local outing

The player takes a trip that is long enough to feel like leaving home but still recoverable.

The route should include:

home
-> familiar edge
-> main road or recognizable path
-> wild pocket
-> objective / discovery
-> decision to continue or return.

The player may:

- fight several routine enemies;
- gather materials;
- make partial progress on more than one intention;
- discover a side path;
- come back with lower health/mana/consumables;
- decide that a second pull is unwise;
- encounter an object/NPC/rumor that creates a future thread rather than an immediate turn-in.

### First high-level threat

During this period, the player should be able to see or accidentally approach **E — the dangerous visible place**.

The threat needs to be legible through one or more of:

- enemy presentation;
- level/readout conventions if retained;
- damage/risk from a cautious approach;
- NPC warning;
- environmental framing;
- obvious concentration of elite/high-level creatures.

The intended memory is:

**"That place on the ridge/other side of the river is not for me yet."**

The player should not be teleported away or prevented from trying.

### End state

It is acceptable to finish this period with unfinished objectives.

The player may simply know more than when they left.

---

# Hour 3–5 — the first route becomes personal

The player repeats part of familiar geography, but the repeat should not be identical.

They may now:

- recognize the road without checking the map;
- leave the road intentionally for gathering or a shortcut;
- understand which enemy pockets to avoid;
- combine one story intention with profession/gathering goals;
- decide whether to spend limited money on training, supplies or repair;
- keep using an item acquired earlier rather than replacing it automatically.

A long-lived quest chain should now have enough context that it clearly extends beyond one outing.

The player should start to feel that some obligations are becoming part of the character's life.

### A remembered item

By this point the character should plausibly have at least one item that is memorable because of:

- where it came from;
- what problem it solved;
- its scarcity;
- its look/name/trait;
- the cost or effort attached to it.

This does not need to be rare-color spectacle.

The item should still be relevant several sessions later unless the player's route produces a genuinely better replacement.

---

# Hour 5–7.5 — first extended expedition

The player prepares for a longer trip.

Preparation may include:

- repairing;
- clearing bag space;
- buying food/drink/reagents;
- training if affordable/appropriate;
- choosing which intentions to pursue;
- choosing safe road versus a more direct/risky route.

The expedition pushes beyond the comfortable local radius.

A healthy structure is:

home
-> known road
-> river/fork landmark
-> unfamiliar stretch
-> dangerous or resource-rich side pocket
-> remote refuge F or forced return.

The trip should include quiet minutes.

Not every bend needs enemies, treasure or scripted events.

### First remote relief

Reaching F should matter because support was far behind.

Even a tiny refuge with only rest, food and limited repair can feel valuable.

The player should feel the difference between:

- being near home;
- being far enough away that continuing carries weight.

### Logout compatibility

This is a natural point where one real-life play session may end.

The player can stop with:

- a long quest unfinished;
- bags partly full;
- a newly learned route;
- tomorrow's destination already in mind.

---

# Hour 7.5–10.5 — reaching the regional settlement

The regional settlement G should feel earned by distance and familiarity accumulated on the way.

The first trip there should generally be physical.

The arrival should provide a meaningful contrast:

- broader services;
- different NPC/faction/profession presence;
- new routes;
- stronger sense of the larger world;
- perhaps transport infrastructure that matters only because the player reached this place first.

The regional settlement should not invalidate home A.

Reasons to keep caring about A may include:

- ongoing NPC relationships;
- unfinished local chain;
- particular trainer/vendor/profession function;
- geographic convenience for another route;
- familiarity;
- storage/inn preference depending on final systems.

### No quest-hub vacuum

The player should not arrive and receive a wall of mandatory task icons.

Some NPCs have no tracked quest.

Some information is rumor.

Some opportunities can wait.

Some content can be unavailable until later circumstances.

### Progression texture

Around this period, the player may have gained several levels, additional class capability, profession familiarity and more money.

But the strongest experiential progress is:

**the world they can confidently operate in has become larger.**

---

# Hour 10.5–13 — first return with knowledge

The player should now have a reason to traverse known space in a way that feels different from the first journey.

Possible structure:

regional settlement
-> known road
-> deliberate shortcut / changed route
-> old local area now slightly easier
-> home settlement
-> turn-in / training / profession / banking / repair / relationship consequence.

The return should demonstrate multiple forms of growth:

- ordinary early enemies are somewhat easier but not necessarily erased;
- navigation requires less attention;
- the player knows where supplies/services are;
- at least one earlier item is still in use or remembered;
- a chain begun several hours ago advances;
- a shortcut or route choice cuts friction through knowledge, not universal teleportation.

### Required emotional beat

**"I know this place now."**

That sentence is more important than a specific XP gain.

---

# Hour 13–15 — the province opens rather than finishes

The first fifteen hours should not end with the starting province exhausted.

Instead, the player should have enough mastery to recognize how much remains.

Possible final-two-hour choices:

- push an existing long chain into a more dangerous stage;
- revisit the dangerous visible place E cautiously and discover it is still beyond reach or only partly manageable;
- prepare for a dungeon/deeper ruin that will take a future expedition;
- work a profession goal using routes learned across the province;
- travel toward H, the deeper direction, without reaching its ultimate destination;
- help an old settlement/NPC for reasons beyond raw XP efficiency;
- choose to spend the session mostly on travel, preparation and unfinished business.

If the player follows the current calibration pace, they may be around level 10.

The level number is not the ending beat.

The ending beat is that the player has become **a resident adventurer of this part of the world rather than a new spawn following markers.**

---

# What should remain unfinished after fifteen hours

At least some of the following should remain:

- the dangerous visible place E;
- one long quest chain;
- one road/direction leading deeper into the continent;
- an NPC or faction relationship with future relevance;
- a profession goal;
- a piece of equipment the player wants but cannot yet obtain;
- a dungeon/elite challenge or equivalent difficult place;
- a shortcut/transport possibility not yet unlocked;
- a reason to return to both home A and regional settlement G.

The exact count is not doctrine.

The principle is:

**the player's mental map should expand faster than their completion map.**

---

# First-fifteen-hours item history test

At the end, ask the player:

1. Which weapon/tool/item do you remember most?
2. Where did it come from?
3. Did you use it for several sessions?
4. Is anything from the first few hours still relevant?

If the answer is "I replaced everything so often I do not know," reward/gear pacing failed.

---

# Quest continuity test

Ask:

1. Which quest/story is still unfinished?
2. Why are you doing it?
3. Who started it?
4. Where does it currently lead?
5. Can the journal reconstruct that context after several real days?

If the player remembers only counters and marker locations, quest design failed.

---

# Settlement relationship test

Ask:

1. What do you use the home settlement for?
2. What do you use the regional settlement for?
3. Which inn/refuge do you remember?
4. Why would you return to an older place after local enemies stop being efficient XP?

If every settlement is functionally interchangeable, settlement doctrine failed.

---

# Mental-map exit test

At approximately fifteen hours, hide the detailed map and ask the player to sketch or verbally describe:

- home A;
- main road C;
- river/fork D;
- dangerous place E;
- refuge F;
- regional settlement G;
- at least one shortcut/alternative path;
- one deeper direction H;
- which routes feel safer and which feel riskier.

The drawing does not need spatial precision.

Success means the player understands **relationships**:

- "the bridge is north of home";
- "the dangerous ruin is above the river road";
- "the outpost is the last safe stop before the long eastern stretch";
- "I can cut through the woods, but the road is safer";
- "the regional town is beyond the crossing, not just a teleport destination."

If the player can list quest locations but cannot reconstruct the province, G7 failed.

---

# What this storyboard does not freeze

It does not freeze:

- province names;
- lore;
- NPC names;
- exact level reached at hour 15;
- exact quest count;
- exact enemy count;
- exact profession unlock timing;
- exact travel minute targets;
- exact gear rewards;
- exact class unlock schedule;
- exact transport mechanics;
- final terrain dimensions.

Those are later authored decisions constrained by this experience.

---

# Rejection rule

A proposed first-province production plan should be rejected or revised if it cannot produce most of these memories without relying on:

- constant objective markers;
- rapid equipment churn;
- artificial XP grinding;
- universal fast travel;
- identical settlements;
- every-minute stimulation;
- tutorialized exposure to every system;
- repeated backtracking inserted only to lengthen playtime.

The opening chapter succeeds when fifteen hours create **familiarity, unfinished desire and geographic memory**, not when fifteen hours clear a content checklist.
